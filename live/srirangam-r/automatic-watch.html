How an automatic watch winds itself

A mechanical watch runs on a coiled spring. An automatic watch keeps that spring wound with a swinging weight, so ordinary wrist movement is enough. Follow the energy from your wrist to the ticking balance. Point at a step to light up its parts in the drawing, or click it to see it work.

  1. 1

    The rotor catches your movement

    The half-moon weight on top spins freely on a ball bearing. When you move your arm, gravity swings its heavy edge round, like a pendulum. Lying flat on a bench it only moves if you push it, so drag it.

  2. 2

    Swinging either way winds the spring

    The rotor turns two small reverser wheels. Each has a one-way clutch, like a bicycle freewheel: one grips while the other slips. So a swing to the left and a swing to the right both turn the next wheels the same way. The gripping wheel's pawls turn bold.

  3. 3

    Gears trade speed for force

    Two reduction wheels slow the motion right down: 140 turns of the rotor turn the big ratchet wheel on the barrel just once. Each rotor turn adds about 3 minutes of running time. The click, the small pawl beside the ratchet, stops the spring unwinding backwards.

  4. 4

    The mainspring stores the energy

    Inside the barrel a long steel spring coils tighter as it is wound. Look through the barrel cover: when wound, the coils hug the centre; when run down, they lie against the outer wall. Fully wound it holds about 42 hours. Right now: 9 h 00.

  5. 5

    It can't be overwound

    The spring's outer end isn't fixed. It presses against the barrel wall and slips round once the spring is full, so extra winding does no harm. Wind it to full and the readout says bridle slipping.

  6. 6

    The gear train lets the energy out slowly

    The spring turns the barrel, the barrel turns the centre wheel once an hour (it carries the minute hand), and further wheels step the speed up: the fourth wheel turns once a minute (the seconds) and drives the small escape wheel.

  7. 7

    The escapement and balance keep time

    Left alone the gears would spin out in seconds. The pallet fork locks the escape wheel and lets it move half a tooth at a time, eight times a second. Each release gives the balance wheel a tiny push, and the balance swings back and forth on its hairspring 4 times a second. That steady swing is the clock. Press space to watch it slowly.

  8. 8

    Or wind it by hand

    Turning the crown drives the same ratchet through the crown wheel; the rotor isn't moved, because the reverser clutches let it slip. About 33 turns of the crown wind it from empty to full. Hold W, or press and hold the crown.

Reading the readout

reserve 9 h 00 is how long the watch would keep running if you put it down now. 261° is how far the balance swings each way from rest. The swing shrinks as the spring runs down and the watch stops when it gets too small, but the beat stays at 4 swings a second, which is why the watch keeps good time all day.
